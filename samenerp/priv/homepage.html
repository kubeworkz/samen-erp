01 — Design flexibility

Shaped like your business, not like a vendor's template.

Most ERPs make you choose between changing your process and forking the product. Samen is built the other way round: the pieces are scopes and declared types you compose, and the surfaces are generated from the same catalog that grounds the AI.

Compose, don't patch

A capability is a scope you mount — not a monolith you hot-fix.

Finance, inventory, procurement, support, CRM and the rest mount into a host with a domain module and a router line. The ERP proof app mounts all of them at roughly zero authored lines of its own.

your product surfacesauthored by you
finance · inventory · procurement · supportmounted
samen_web — planes, masking, UI kitframework
samen_core — vault, audit, policies, generatorskernel
Fields on a Tuesday

Add the field your business actually needs, at runtime.

Custom fields live in a validated JSON bag with bounded types — string, integer, number, boolean, date, enum, money, url, phone, email, address. Vaulted values are validated and normalised on the write path, not on a hope.

stringintegernumber booleandateenum moneyurlphone emailaddress

Plus declared rich types: Money, Percent, Score, Duration, Priority, URL, Email, Phone, Address.

Generate a vertical

One command emits a running product — and it passes its own gate first run.

The generator produces the web UI, JSON:API, seeds, observability, tests and a 19-step verifier gate. No hand edits, no scaffolding graveyard.

# emit a new vertical $ mix samen.gen.app --module Harbor --prefix hb --abbrev hrb # it boots, serves every mounted route, # and its verifier gate is green on the first run
One catalog, every surface

The schema is the source of truth for your app, your API and your assistant.

A machine-readable catalog describes every model and field. It renders the in-product documentation, keeps the OpenAPI surface honest, and grounds LLM tooling in your real schema instead of a plausible-sounding guess.

02 — Durability

Durability is a property of the ledger, not a line in the brochure.

A flexible ERP is only worth having if it holds under a bad Tuesday. These are structural refusals, not settings: the wrong thing cannot be recorded in the first place.

R1 · General ledger

An unbalanced entry cannot be posted.

Double entry refuses it by construction — not a validation flag somebody can turn off before month-end.

R3 · Stock

Stock cannot go negative.

The append-only stock ledger is the source of truth and the on-hand rollup is derived from it. The red path fails closed.

R5 · Procurement

A receipt posts stock and the GL together.

One transaction, three-way match enforced. A half-posted goods receipt cannot exist to be cleaned up later.

Audit

The audit chain cannot be quietly edited.

Hash-chained and append-only, with a database trigger that refuses UPDATE and DELETE — and the tenant can read the chain the operator writes to.

Erasure

Erasure is key destruction, not row scrubbing.

Shred a subject's key material and their vaulted PII is unrecoverable across every tier — attested by a separate-process destruction oracle.

Integrations

Adapters fail honestly.

An unconfigured integration returns :not_configured, never success. Billing and email adapters cannot claim work they did not do.

Every guarantee ships with a sabotage that proves its test can fail.

A test that cannot fail is treated as a bug in this codebase. 308 committed patches replay against the tree, each one required to break the exact test that claims the guarantee — then the tree is restored and checked byte for byte.

03 — Integrated AI

AI that reads your schema, not a screenshot of it.

The AI plane is part of the kernel, not a chat box bolted onto a settings tab. It is bring-your-own-key, grounded in the catalog, and bound by the same vault rules as every other surface.

BYOK

Your key, encrypted at rest.

HuggingFace as the BYOK provider, with AES-256-GCM key encryption and token validation. No Samen account in the middle of your inference bill.

Streaming

SSE streaming, not a spinner.

Token streaming and usage analytics are covered by 109 tests — 59 on the core and 50 on streaming integration.

Grounding

The catalog is the context.

Assistants reason over the machine-readable schema dictionary, so “add a PO for that vendor” maps to real resources and real policies.

Privacy

Masked stays masked.

PII resolves through the vault plane. Agent and support surfaces render •••• unless a scoped, second-party-approved reveal grant exists — and every reveal lands in the audit chain.

04 — Enterprise

Two planes, governed reveals, and a vendor-free kernel.

Enterprise features here are architecture, not an upsell. The tenant product plane and the operator control plane are both masked by default, and every disclosure is a second-party decision with an expiry and a receipt.

Plane 01

Tenant product plane

Your team's workspace: finance, procurement, inventory, support, CRM, billing, work.

PII vault
hash-chained audit
Plane 02

Operator control plane

Support and operations, masked by default — including impersonation sessions.

impersonationreveal grantskill switchesaggregates
Reveal

Second-party by construction

A distinct approver, enforced in policy and by a database CHECK. Grants expire and cannot be renewed in place.

Privacy

PII vaulted on both planes

One vault chokepoint, vt_ tokens at rest, masked rendering everywhere.

Audit

An impersonated write without an audit row is impossible

Audit-on-write has the impersonation context as its mandatory first client.

Billing

Stripe as an adapter, not a dependency

The kernel names zero Stripe strings; the adapter lives in a sibling package with hosted checkout and fail-closed webhooks.

Email

One behaviour, three vendors

Postmark, SES and Resend satisfy the same conformance harness. PII-safe rendering with a no-leak gate.

Identity

Self-serve spine included

Signup, verification, reset, sessions, invites, OIDC with TOTP step-up, and onboarding — generated, not stubbed.

Lifecycle

Approvals you can point at

A general approve/reject engine with requester ≠ approver, plus archival and versioning on the same substrate.

Ops

Deploy like software

Docker and Compose, a Fly / ECS / Kubernetes guide, health and readiness probes, and a gated release path.

05 — Open source

All of it. MIT, no asterisk.

The kernel, the web layer, the generators and the ERP are in one repository you can clone right now. Every claim on this page links to a test, a verifier, a probe or a checked-in artifact — and the claims we cannot prove are listed as unwired, not quietly rounded up.

06 — The claim set

What's live, what's a shell, and what we refuse to claim.

The same discipline that writes the tests writes the marketing. This is the short version; the long version is the README's claim table.

Live and tested

  • PII vault, masked rendering on both planes, crypto-shred erasure with a destruction oracle
  • Second-party reveal grants and the hash-chained, tenant-readable audit log
  • Identity spine: signup, verification, reset, sessions, invites, OIDC + TOTP, 2FA
  • Finance GL, AP/AR, stock ledger, procurement, manufacturing, HR/leave, expenses, banking, multi-currency, statements
  • Stripe billing and Postmark / SES / Resend delivery behind one behaviour
  • HuggingFace BYOK AI core: encrypted keys, validation, completion, SSE, usage analytics

Shell, not wired

  • HuggingFace settings key flow and the daily credential sweep
  • SSO / SAML production assertion validation
  • Monitoring, automated backups and PITR
  • Plan-based API and AI rate limits
  • Customer audit-log export, data residency, white-label branding
  • Warehouse transfers and landed-cost posting workflows

Kept out on purpose

  • “Run support without ever seeing PII” — retired as false by construction
  • “Complete API reference” — the served OpenAPI covers two paths today
  • Trial Balance as a live statement — deleted rather than decorated
  • Compliance certification — we describe controls, we do not claim an audit
